RESEARCH

Understanding how demographic and socio-economic systems evolve, interact and respond to policy.

I use System Dynamics and complementary quantitative and foresight methods to study population change, labour, welfare, energy and sustainability as interconnected systems.

Rather than treating demographic or economic trends in isolation, I focus on feedback, adaptation, path dependence and unintended consequences over time.

RESEARCH AREAS

Three connected lines of inquiry

Demographic Dynamics

I study fertility, migration, population ageing and changing population structures as dynamic processes shaped by behaviour, values, economic conditions and policy.

How do fertility and migration respond to changing social and economic conditions? How do demographic processes reinforce one another over time? What makes population trajectories difficult to reverse?

Socio-Economic Systems

My work examines how demographic change interacts with labour supply, retirement, pensions, welfare institutions, public finance and other socio-economic structures.

How do institutions adapt to demographic pressure? When do policies complement or undermine one another? How can feedback create policy resistance, delays or unintended distributional effects?

Sustainability Transitions

I extend systems approaches to questions of energy, resources, behaviour and policy, with an interest in resilience and the long-term consequences of transition strategies.

How do resource constraints and behavioural responses interact? Where do cross-sector effects emerge? Which policy combinations improve resilience without shifting problems elsewhere?

HOW I STUDY COMPLEX SYSTEMS

Methods & analytical approaches

System Dynamics feedback, stocks and flows, simulation
Systems Thinking causal structure and problem framing
Quantitative Modelling data analysis, estimation and calibration
Foresight drivers, uncertainty and alternative futures
Scenario Analysis policy pathways and long-term behaviour
Policy Simulation interaction, trade-offs and unintended effects

I treat models as tools for explanation and learning, not only prediction. The aim is to make assumptions explicit, connect evidence with causal reasoning and explore why systems behave as they do under different interventions.
